Figure 2. The architecture of our proposed MMDIR. Our model handles multimodal inputs and outputs for document image restoration.
In training stage as shown in the (a) on the left, it takes a Degraded Image Id , a corresponding Question (Text Instruction) and Answer
(Degradation Identification) as input. The vision encoder and text embedding first extract multimodal tokens (V, Q, A), which are then
fed into a Large Language Model (LLM) to learn cross-modal feature alignment. During the inference stage (in the (b)), only the Id and
the Question are used as input to the model. In the Question and Answer, degradation types that are not present in the input image are
marked in red, while those actually present are highlighted in green. Other types requiring model identification are labeled in orange. LLM
generates text responses by decoding step by step to understand the content of instructions and to identify specific degradation types in the
input image. The vision decoder incorporates the semantically enriched answer features from the LLM’s output as guidance. It ultimately
reconstructs the restored images (Ir ).
